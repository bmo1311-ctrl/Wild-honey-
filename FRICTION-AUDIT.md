# Where the app is asking too much

An audit of every member-facing surface, measured rather than guessed.
September 7, 2026.

---

## The one finding that matters

**The daily check-in asks eight questions, every single day.**

Three sliders (energy, sleep quality, stress), cycle phase, mood as free
text, and then four number fields: hydration in ounces, protein in grams,
sunlight in minutes, movement in minutes.

Four of those require her to estimate a quantity she probably has not
measured. And the protein one is asking twice — the food log already
computes protein from what she ate.

This is the burnout engine. Not the courses. A woman will do eight fields
on day one because she is excited, five on day four, and on day nine she
will not open the app, because opening it means being interviewed.

**What I would do:** three taps is the whole check-in — energy, sleep,
stress. Everything else moves behind an optional "add more" that most days
she never touches. Protein and movement come from what she already logged
rather than being asked for again.

That takes the daily cost from about ninety seconds to about eight.

## Friction, ranked

### 1. Today can ask for five things before it asks for a single habit

On a normal morning Today generates rows for: the course day, log how you
feel, log what you ate, the weekly weigh-in, the money check-in — then one
row per habit. A member with five habits sees ten things to do.

Nothing caps it and nothing ranks it. Every row looks equally urgent, so
none of them reads as *the* thing.

**Fix:** show at most three, chosen by what actually matters today, with
the rest behind "anything else". The day's work is one of the three.

### 2. Onboarding is five steps before she sees anything

`you → focus → body → rhythm → fit`. She paid, she arrived, and the app
asks her five screens of questions before showing her a single thing she
came for.

**Fix:** name only, then straight in. The other four get asked later, in
context, at the moment they would actually change something — or inferred,
the way the wash-day engine learns her rhythm instead of asking.

### 3. The food logger has thirteen inputs

The heaviest screen in the app, and one she is meant to use several times
a day.

**Fix:** worth a session on its own. The fastest win is that logging the
same thing again should be one tap, since most people eat the same
fifteen things.

### 4. Thirty-nine member-facing pages

Archive, ask, becoming, body, calendar, challenges, checkin, circle,
community, energy, fitness, groups, guidelines, household, kid-food,
kid-me, kid-money, learning, library, members, membership, money,
nutrition, pantry, profile, program, progress, protocols, recipes,
retreats, settings, shop, vault, and more.

Any one of them is defensible. Together they are a building with
thirty-nine doors and no lobby.

**Fix:** not deletion — most of this is good work. But the Library needs
to answer "what is in here" in one screen, and several of these should
live inside another page rather than beside it.

## One course at a time

Half of this is already true, which is worth knowing before changing
anything.

**Already right:** Today shows exactly one course — the active
enrollment — not all four. And the Programs page skips itself entirely
and sends her straight to the course when she only has one.

**What is actually wrong:** the course switcher sits on Today. It is a
reminder, every morning, that there are other programs she is not doing.
That is the overwhelm you felt, and it is one component in one place.

**What I would do, which is close to what you described:**

- Take the switcher off Today. Today carries one course, silently.
- Put the flag on the Programs page instead: each course card shows what
  is waiting inside it — *day 12 ready*, *4 days behind*, *finished* —
  so the pending work is visible where she goes looking for it, and
  invisible where she is trying to do today's thing.
- One course is the primary one. Others stay enrolled and keep their
  progress; they just stop competing for the morning.

## Making it feel like a game

The bones already exist and are barely felt: streaks, milestones at 1, 7,
14 and 21 days, the Becoming page, challenges, the wins journal.

None of them has a moment. Nothing celebrates. A milestone is a row in a
list she has to go and look at.

**What would actually work here:**

- **A moment when a day is finished.** Right now completing a day is a
  button going quiet. That is the single highest-value place to put
  delight, because it is the exact instant she has earned something.
- **Show the next milestone, close up.** "Two days to one week" does more
  than a badge she already has.
- **Make the Becoming page the reward.** Evidence of change she can see —
  that is a stronger pull than points, and you already compute it.

**One honest caution.** Points, leaderboards and streaks-that-punish would
work against what this app is for. Your skincare engine deliberately
enforces rest nights. Your noticing engine counts what she did and never
what she missed. A streak that shames a gap contradicts both, and with
women already prone to all-or-nothing it will produce the quitting you
are trying to prevent.

So: **celebrate, do not score.** Make finishing feel good. Never make
missing feel bad. Everything above holds to that.

---

## If I did three things

1. **Cut the daily check-in to three taps.** Biggest effect on whether
   she is still here in week three, and the smallest change on this list.
2. **Cap Today at three things.** It is the difference between a to-do
   list and a demand.
3. **Give finishing a day a real moment.** The cheapest joy in the whole
   app, at the moment she has earned it.

Onboarding, the food logger, and the page sprawl are all real, and all
bigger jobs. They can wait.
