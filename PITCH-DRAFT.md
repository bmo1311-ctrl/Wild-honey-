# The pitch, rewritten

Read it, cut what isn't you, and tell me what to ship. Nothing here is live yet.

---

## What's wrong with the pitch you have

It sells a feeling. *"Show up softly. Grow wildly. A whole-life practice for
women."* Lovely, and it could be a candle.

It doesn't say the one thing that made your friend's face change on that
hike: **she is paying for four apps that don't talk to each other, and this
is one that does.** That's not a soft benefit. It's the reason to switch.

And it hides the part that's genuinely hard to copy. The app doesn't just
*hold* her data — it knows things. It won't let her use retinol two nights
running. It alternates protein and moisture in her hair so it doesn't snap.
It tracks vitamins and minerals against where she is in her cycle. It flags
that two things on her shelf cancel each other out. None of that is on the
page.

---

## The line

> **Everything you're tracking in five apps, in one that actually understands
> how it fits together.**

Plainer than what's there, and it's the sentence that does the work.

Keep *Start with the body. The rest of the life is in here.* underneath it —
that line is good and it's yours.

## Hero — three ways

**A. The one your friend needs to hear**
> ### You are keeping this in five apps. None of them talk to each other.
> Your cycle in one. Your food in another. A skincare routine you're guessing
> at. A journal you stopped opening. Wild Honey holds all of it — and knows
> how one affects the next.

**B. Warmer, still concrete**
> ### She knows what your Tuesday needs before you do.
> Where you are in your cycle, what you ate, what your skin can take tonight,
> what you wrote when the week last looked like this. One place, and it
> remembers.

**C. Closest to your current voice**
> ### Start with the body. The rest of the life is in here.
> Not five apps that each take a piece of you. One that holds your cycle,
> your food, your skin, your hair, your money and your writing — and
> understands how they move together.

I'd ship **C**. It keeps your line, adds the argument, and doesn't sound
like a productivity app shouting.

## The section that's missing

This is the "depth of intuitive education" you're after — and the honest way
to say it is to *show* it rather than claim it. Four examples do more than
any adjective.

> ## It doesn't just hold your notes. It knows the rules.
>
> Most apps are a container. You put things in, and later you look at them.
> This one has the reasoning built in — so you learn why, instead of
> guessing.
>
> **Your skin gets nights off, whether you ask for them or not.**
> Retinol needs two nights between. Acids need longer. Do them back to back
> and you spend three weeks repairing a barrier. Wild Honey plans the week so
> that can't happen, and tells you what tonight is for.
>
> **Your hair counts washes, not days.**
> Protein and moisture have to alternate — protein twice running is how hair
> goes stiff, then brittle, then short. It knows the difference between a
> bond builder and a mask, even when the bottle doesn't.
>
> **Your food is read down to the vitamin.**
> Not calories and three macros. Iron, magnesium, folate, B12, K — hundreds
> of foods, from figs to the Costco hot dog, with the numbers behind them.
>
> **It tells you when two things on your shelf are fighting.**
> Vitamin C and benzoyl peroxide. A retinoid stacked on an acid. Things
> worth knowing before you're pregnant, and worth asking your doctor about
> when you are.

## Where the pitch is now dishonest

Worth fixing whatever else you keep.

The page says **"Three programs"** and **"both programs"** in different
places. There are four courses. Somebody counting will notice.

It says **"A library that learns you — the shelves reorder around what you
actually watch."** Check that's true before it ships. If the shelves don't
actually reorder, that sentence has to go; a small false claim on a sales
page costs more than the sentence is worth.

---

## What I deliberately didn't write

You said be careful, so here's what I avoided and why. Push back on any of it.

**No health outcomes.** Nothing about clearer skin, better sleep, weight,
hormones or energy. You'd be a membership app making medical claims, and the
one thing you can't buy back is a woman who believed a promise.

**No "transformation in 30 days."** Your own app enforces rest nights and
refuses to shame a gap. A pitch that promises fast change contradicts the
product on the inside — and she'll feel that in week two.

**No "clean."** You already threw that out, rightly.

**Nothing about AI or intelligence as a buzzword.** The reasoning is real, so
it's stronger to show four specific rules than to say "smart" once.

**No comparison to named apps.** Tempting — but naming competitors ages badly
and invites a fight you don't need.
